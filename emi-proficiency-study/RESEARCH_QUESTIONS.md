# Research questions and design

**Working title.** English-Medium Instruction and Oral–Aural Attrition: An Explanatory Mixed-Methods Study of Engineering Students’ Proficiency Change

**Design.** Explanatory sequential mixed methods (QUANT → QUAL).  
The quantitative phase tests whether institutional English proficiency changes from Preparatory Year Programme (PYP) exit to graduation. The qualitative phase then explains any oral–aural attrition through lecturer and student accounts of classroom language use (especially translanguaging) in the EMI setting.

Scores are on a **0–100** institutional scale; PYP pass threshold = **60** (≈ CEFR B1).

---

## Research questions

### RQ1 (Quantitative)

To what extent do students’ institutional English proficiency scores change from PYP exit to graduation after four years of engineering EMI, across Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking, and Overall?

*Anticipated pattern (synthetic panel for scaffolding):* statistically significant decline in **Listening** and **Speaking**; no statistically significant decline in **Reading** or **Writing** (slight non-significant gains possible from written academic exposure).

### RQ2 (Qualitative — lecturers)

How do content lecturers describe whether and why they use translanguaging (Turkish alongside English) in EMI courses?

*Anticipated themes:* translanguaging as a compensatory strategy when lecturers judge students’ English insufficient for content learning; securing content knowledge even at the expense of English-medium interaction.

### RQ3 (Qualitative — students)

How do students experience EMI classroom language practices, and how do they relate those practices to changes in their English proficiency—especially Listening and Speaking?

*Anticipated themes:*
- some students report that lecturers’ English and/or course materials (syllabi, books) exceed their proficiency and impede comprehension;
- other students report that lecturers’ own English is not strong enough for sustained EMI teaching;
- in either case, limited verbal interaction in English and frequent translanguaging are linked to a felt decline in oral–aural proficiency (“not that good, and getting worse”).

---

## What is quantitative vs qualitative

| Strand | Data | Analysis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Quantitative (this phase)** | Paired institutional proficiency scores (Pre PYP exit / Post graduation) for four skills + Overall | Descriptives; paired-samples *t* (Wilcoxon companion); effect sizes / CIs; one Pre–Post figure |
| **Qualitative (next phase)** | Lecturer interviews/accounts; student interviews/accounts | Thematic analysis explaining *why* oral–aural attrition occurs (translanguaging, proficiency attributions, materials, lecturer English) |

**Out of the quantitative strand (deferred / not analysed statistically here):** underrating gap, TL%, Likert mechanism scales, OLS/mediation, Golem path models. Those constructs may inform qualitative coding later but are not quantitative research questions.

---

## Analytic sample (quantitative)

- *N* = 120 EMI engineering completers (synthetic panel for method scaffolding; replace with live institutional data).
- Same students at Pre and Post.
- Majors: Mechanical, Chemical, Electrical-Electronics (balanced).
