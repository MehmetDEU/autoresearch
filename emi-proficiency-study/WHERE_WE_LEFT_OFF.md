### Qualitative questions
- **RQ2 (students, open, first).** To what do students attribute their oral–aural attrition?
- **Follow-up, not an RQ.** *Why do you think lecturers use Turkish so often?* Semi-structured. Ask after the open answer, of each student. Prepared because the researcher observed frequent lecturer L1 in these classrooms over several years. If the student already mentioned Turkish, probe that remark. If not, ask it anyway and report it as planned.
- **RQ3 (lecturers).** When you use L1, what underlies that use?

### Themes and interpretation (expected, not yet found)
- Student follow-up, two themes only: (1) lecturers’ limited English; (2) underrating of students’ English. Content security and shared Turkish sit inside (2), not as a third theme.
- Open RQ2 may also yield reliance on written technical discourse (diagrams, formulae, slides inside that theme, not a separate multimodality theme) and peer talk in Turkish.
- Lecturers are expected to cite **only students’ low proficiency**, not their own English. That answer is the **Golem effect** (Babad, Inbar, & Rosenthal, 1982). Qualitative name only. No Golem regression.
- Lecturers are not expected to reflect on their own spoken English. Expected excuse: they already validated English for EMI via **YDS or YÖKDİL** (ÖSYM). Those tests are reading-based multiple choice (grammar, vocabulary, translation, textual meaning) and have **no Listening or Speaking**. A pass does not document oral–aural EMI proficiency. Use this beside lecturer excerpts. It is not the Golem effect.
