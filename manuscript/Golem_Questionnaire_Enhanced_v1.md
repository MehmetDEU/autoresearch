# Perceived External Discouragement and Medium-of-Instruction Choice: Enhanced Questionnaire (v1)

*Instrument for Turkish university students who prefer Turkish-medium instruction (TMI) over English-medium instruction (EMI). Aligns with the reframed construct in `Golem_reframed_v1.md` and extends the original 21-item TMI-over-EMI scale (see `Golem_v8_shortened.md`, Appendix).*

---

## Instrument overview

This questionnaire measures **why students prefer TMI over EMI**, with particular attention to **perceived external discouragement**—the dissuasive advice and lowered expectations students recall from significant others before or during university choice.

The social influence block is organised around **four theoretically distinct lenses** drawn from the reframed introduction. Each lens is operationalised as a subscale; together they explain *how* social pressure is experienced, not only *that* it occurred.

| Subscale | Construct | Primary sources |
|----------|-----------|-----------------|
| **A. Controlled motivation (MOI choice)** | Externally pressured or guilt-driven medium-of-instruction choice, as distinct from self-endorsed preference | Ryan & Connell (1989); Black & Deci (2000); Ryan & Deci (2000, 2020) |
| **B. Ought-to L2 self (avoidance)** | Others' expectations shaping the learner one feels one *ought* to become—or avoid becoming—in relation to English | Dörnyei (2009); Taguchi et al. (2009); Papi (2010); You & Dörnyei (2016) |
| **C. Interdependent self-construal (educational deference)** | Relational self; deference to family, elders, and teachers in major educational decisions | Singelis (1994); Cross et al. (2011); Markus & Kitayama (1991) |
| **D. Perceived linguistic capital deficit** | English/EMI as prestige capital; social sorting; beliefs that EMI is not meant for one's social position | Bourdieu (1986, 1991); Li (2012); Altay (2025) |
| **E. Perceived external discouragement** | Recalled dissuasive messages and lowered horizons from significant others (outcome-focused; overlaps with B and A but kept distinct) | Original Golem-proxy items (items 19–21); author-built extensions |

**Section F** retains six **practical/individual decision factors** from the original instrument (18 items) so that RQ1 can compare social and non-social influences.

**Response format (Sections B–F):** 5-point Likert scale  
1 = *strongly disagree / no impact on my decision* · 2 = *disagree* · 3 = *neither agree nor disagree* · 4 = *agree* · 5 = *strongly agree / key reason for my decision*

Items marked **[R]** are reverse-coded when scored toward the named subscale.

## Section 2. Social influence subscales

*Instructions:* The following statements concern your decision to study in **Turkish** rather than **English** at university. Indicate how much each statement applied to you **at the time you made that decision**.

### A. Controlled motivation in medium-of-instruction choice [SDT-C]  
*Adapted from Ryan & Connell (1989) external and introjected regulation; Black & Deci (2000) autonomy-support framework, recast for MOI choice.*

| ID | Item | Tag |
|----|------|-----|
| SDT-C1 | I chose TMI mainly because parents, teachers, or advisors expected me to—not because I personally preferred it. | SDT-C |
| SDT-C2 | When deciding on the medium of instruction, I felt **pressured** by others' views rather than free to choose for myself. | SDT-C |
| SDT-C3 | I would have felt **guilty** if I had chosen EMI against the advice of family or teachers. | SDT-C |
| SDT-C4 | Significant others made me feel that choosing EMI would **disappoint** people who matter to me. | SDT-C |
| SDT-C5 | Choosing TMI felt like a decision I had **genuinely made on my own**, without pressure from others. **[R]** | SDT-C |

### B. Ought-to L2 self (avoidance) [O2]  
*Adapted from Taguchi et al. (2009), Papi (2010), and You & Dörnyei (2016) ought-to L2 self items; wording shifted from general L2 motivation to MOI avoidance.*

| ID | Item | Tag |
|----|------|-----|
| O2-1 | I felt I **ought** to study in Turkish because that is what people important to me expected. | O2 |
| O2-2 | Others shaped the kind of future I felt I was **supposed** to pursue, and that future did not require EMI. | O2 |
| O2-3 | Significant others made me believe that EMI was **not the right path for someone like me**. | O2 |
| O2-4 | I felt I **should avoid** EMI in order to meet the expectations of family or teachers. | O2 |
| O2-5 | The English-using professional others expect me to become is **not** someone who needs an EMI degree. | O2 |

### C. Interdependent self-construal in educational decisions [ISC]  
*Adapted from Singelis (1994) Interdependent Self-Construal Scale; contextualised to university MOI choice per Cross et al. (2011).*

| ID | Item | Tag |
|----|------|-----|
| ISC-1 | It was **important** for me to take my family's wishes into account when choosing the medium of instruction. | ISC |
| ISC-2 | I **deferred to the judgment** of elders (parents, grandparents, senior relatives) in this decision. | ISC |
| ISC-3 | My sense of who I am is closely tied to my **role within my family**, and that role pointed toward TMI. | ISC |
| ISC-4 | Respecting the educational guidance of **parents and teachers** is part of how I make major decisions in Türkiye. | ISC |
| ISC-5 | I chose TMI **without much regard** for what my family thought. **[R]** | ISC |

### D. Perceived linguistic capital deficit [LC]  
*Author-built items informed by Bourdieu (1986, 1991), Li (2012), and Turkish EMI context (Altay, 2025; interview data in Golem manuscripts). No direct published MOI scale exists; treat as exploratory.*

| ID | Item | Tag |
|----|------|-----|
| LC-1 | English-medium study is widely seen as a **marker of prestige and social status** in Türkiye. | LC |
| LC-2 | I had the sense that EMI programmes were mainly for students from **more privileged** backgrounds. | LC |
| LC-3 | People around me implied that EMI was **"not for families like ours."** | LC |
| LC-4 | Choosing EMI seemed to require kinds of **cultural and economic capital** my family did not have. | LC |
| LC-5 | Significant others treated English-medium degrees as valuable mainly for people aiming at **elite or international** careers—not for people in my situation. | LC |

### E. Perceived external discouragement [PED]  
*Three items adapted from the original External Discouraging Expectations subscale (items 19–21); two author-built extensions emphasising recalled advice rather than internalised ought/pressure.*

| ID | Item | Tag | Source |
|----|------|-----|--------|
| PED-1 | My family **discouraged** me from choosing EMI by suggesting it would be too risky or unsuitable for me. | PED | Adapted (orig. item 19) |
| PED-2 | A teacher or educational advisor suggested that EMI would be **too difficult** for me because of my English level or academic background. | PED | Adapted (orig. item 20) |
| PED-3 | **Negative expectations** from others influenced my decision by making TMI seem safer and more realistic than EMI. | PED | Adapted (orig. item 21) |
| PED-4 | Someone I **trusted explicitly advised me against** applying to an EMI programme. | PED | Author-built |
| PED-5 | I recall being told, in one way or another, to **lower my ambitions** regarding English-medium study. | PED | Author-built |

## Section 5. Open-ended prompts

*(Qualitative complement; aligns with interview themes: socioeconomic reach, academic risk, occupational necessity.)*

**O1.** In your own words, what were the **main reasons** you chose TMI rather than EMI?

**O2.** Did anyone important to you **discourage** EMI? If yes, **who** were they, and what did they say? *(You may write in Turkish or English.)*

**O3.** Was there a particular **sentence or piece of advice** that stayed with you and affected your decision? Please quote or paraphrase it as closely as you remember.

**O4.** Looking back, do you think the people who advised you **accurately assessed** your ability to succeed in EMI? Why or why not?

## Psychometric validation plan

Before main administration:

1. **Pilot (*n* ≈ 50–80):** Item clarity, completion time (target ≤20 minutes), floor/ceiling effects. Cognitive debriefing with 5–10 students.
2. **Exploratory factor analysis (EFA):** On the 25 social items (Sections 2A–2E), expect 4–6 factors. Compare varimax and oblimin rotation; KMO ≥ .70; Bartlett's *p* < .001. Test whether PED loads separately from O2 and SDT-C.
3. **Reliability:** Cronbach's α ≥ .70 per subscale; report McDonald's ω if items show varying loadings.
4. **Practical-factor block:** Confirm six-factor structure as in original study (α = .80–.85 per subscale; overall α ≈ .91).
5. **Convergent/discriminant validity:** Predetermined expectations—PED correlates moderately–highly with O2 and SDT-C (*r* ≈ .40–.65) but should not be redundant (average inter-item correlation < .70 across scales); LC correlates with F5 (financial) but should retain distinct variance; ISC correlates with parental influence on S1–S3.
6. **Criterion validity:** Interview subsample (*n* ≈ 30–35) selected on high PED + high score on at least one social subscale; member-check subscale labels against narrative themes.

*Re-administration note:* If this instrument replaces the original 21-item scale, prior *N* = 308 findings serve as historical benchmark only; report enhanced-instrument psychometrics separately.
